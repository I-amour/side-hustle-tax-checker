The original message stays exactly where it was. The translation sits underneath it, quietly, with the language it came from. Nothing is ever replaced. Machine translation gets things wrong sometimes, and plenty of people using this are learning the language, so hiding the original would be taking something away.

And notice the --force in the second message. Code, links, mentions, custom emoji and slash commands are left completely alone. Half of Discord is dev servers, and a translated code block is worse than no translation at all.

Why every other one runs out

Have a look at the reviews on the alternatives and you will see the same three complaints over and over: a daily limit nobody told you about, a paywall that appears mid-conversation, and the thing quietly breaking.

That is not laziness, it is arithmetic. They send every message to a cloud translation service that charges them per translation, so every user costs them money and rationing is the only way the sums work.

Earshot uses the translator Chrome now ships inside the browser itself. The model downloads once and lives on your machine. There is no per-message cost, which is why there is no per-message limit. Unlimited is not generosity here, it is just how it works.

Nothing to ration

No cloud call means no cost per message, which means no quota to hide from you in the small print.

Nothing to leak

Your conversations never leave the machine. Not to me, not to anyone. There is no server to send them to.

What you need

Being straight with you before you install, because there is nothing worse than a tool that installs happily and then silently does nothing:

  • Chrome 138 or newer, on desktop. These browser APIs do not exist on phones or tablets yet.
  • Around 22GB of free disk space, which is Chrome's requirement rather than mine.
  • A one-time language pack download the first time you meet a new language. After that it is instant, and works offline.
  • Roughly 39 languages supported so far, which is Chrome's current list and grows over time.

If your setup cannot do it, Earshot will tell you plainly when you open it, rather than pretending.
